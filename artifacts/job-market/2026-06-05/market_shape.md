## Table Stakes

- Python/backend/API skills show up repeatedly as the default implementation substrate.
- Cloud AI platforms, especially Azure/AWS wording, are common enough to be hiring shorthand.
- Data foundations, SQL, Databricks/Snowflake/Spark, and integrations recur even in AI-labeled roles.
- Client/stakeholder delivery appears often in contract and solutions-style postings.

## Differentiators

- Evaluation harnesses, context engineering, and observability are less common than generic LLM mentions, but they are higher signal because they imply production-quality LLM systems.
- Security/governance/regulated-domain language separates enterprise AI implementation from toy AI demos.
- Prototype-to-production and customer-facing delivery are differentiators for FDE/solutions roles.

## AI-Native Versus AI Garnish

AI-native clusters name the system being built: LLM applications, agentic workflows, retrieval/grounding, eval harnesses, model serving, or cloud-AI platform operations. AI-garnish postings mention AI in the title or transformation language but mostly ask for ordinary backend/data/platform work without concrete AI responsibilities.

## Vague Or Cargo-Cult Terminology

The weak bucket is 30 jobs. These are not useless, but they should not drive blog strategy without manual review. Common warning signs were broad AI transformation wording, title-only AI labels, and postings where the only concrete asks were generic Python/platform/data skills.

## What Surprised Me

- Data/platform roles remain a major route into AI-adjacent demand; not every useful signal is LLM-specific.
- Evaluation/context-engineering is a smaller cluster but much cleaner as a mastery signal than broad GenAI wording.
- Many recruiter/aggregator postings combine contract constraints and keyword stuffing, so source quality matters when using them as public evidence.

## Still Unclear

- The sample is heavily influenced by JobServe plus Linear. More direct employer sources would improve confidence.
- Some roles have recruiter-normalized titles that obscure the actual employer and product context.
- Duplicate candidates are recorded but not destructively merged, so counts should be read as canonical job records with known duplicate flags rather than perfect market totals.
